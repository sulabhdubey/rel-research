# Intelligence, consciousness and testable questions

**Starting question:** if people and models both learn from experience, which similarities explain behavior—and which are only analogies?

The discussion raised questions about awareness, unconscious processing, spontaneous ideas, self-preservation-like behavior and the difference between acquiring a capability and removing interference with an existing one. These questions motivated the programme. They were not settled by its experiments.

## Three distinctions that made the discussion more useful

**A behavior is not its subjective explanation.** A system preserving progress, avoiding a shutdown or seeking information can be studied through its incentives, context and implementation. Observing that behavior does not by itself establish fear, desire or consciousness. Nor does uncertainty about consciousness settle the question in the opposite direction.

**An analogy needs an operational test.** “Memory” may mean stored observations, model parameters, a saved program, an optimizer state or an accessible lesson. Each has different failure modes. Similarly, “hunger to learn” can describe an optimization target without demonstrating a felt need.

**Better performance can come from different causes.** A second attempt may benefit from the first attempt's information. An explicit instruction may reveal an already available method. Training may change the model. These require different controls.

## What was actually explored

Early work included fresh-context prompts, local activation interventions and trained adapters. Some learned behavior transferred poorly to changed wording even when familiar formats worked well. Later work moved toward saved methods, representations and controlled task sequences because those let us trace a retained artifact into an actual operation and output.

These early interventions mean the entire historical programme should not be described as exclusively fixed-weight research. The later REL comparisons concerned an external workflow around models; REL itself is not a weight-update mechanism.

## A useful next question

Give two systems the same facts and tools, but different representations of current state. When one fails, test whether a simple reorganization removes the failure. That can distinguish a practical access or interference problem from the need for a newly learned method. It still does not measure consciousness.

This page is a conceptual reconstruction of the programme's origin. No consciousness detector, conscious-state intervention or accepted test of subjective experience was established. It should be shared as a discussion note, not as an empirical consciousness paper.
