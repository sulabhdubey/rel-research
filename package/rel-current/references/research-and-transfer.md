# Research and transfer checks

Use these checks when a cycle depends on multiple sources, changing facts, accumulated prior work, or a claim that a retained lesson improved the outcome. Keep ordinary low-risk cycles brief.

1. Frame the next decision as a question with at least one answer that would change the action. Read the current artifact and last cycle record first; avoid replaying searches whose relevant evidence has not changed.
2. Select sources for that question. Prefer directly observable task evidence and primary or authoritative documentation for factual claims; compare independent sources when consequences, uncertainty, or disagreement warrant it. Record source version/date, exact claim and assumptions, what remains unverified, and a validity window when source changes could alter a consequential action. Recheck such a source at the point of action. Browsing is valuable for live or niche claims but does not replace local measurement.
3. Distinguish **discovery** from **verification**. A model-generated critique, search result, or self-made practice case can suggest a new path; it cannot by itself confirm the path works. A direct exploratory observation can settle a bounded feasibility or interface question within its observed scope; it does not establish general benefit. Use an external check, reproducible measurement, authorized user feedback, or an independent evaluator suited to the claim. For an empirical comparison, keep confirmation cases independent of exposed development feedback.
4. Preserve a compact working state: the primary user outcome and direct evidence toward it, cumulative cost, material cycles without direct outcome evidence even when local decisions change, verified findings, unresolved constraints, source and environment dependencies, failed approaches, and the next decision-changing observation. At the next cycle, explicitly decide whether each relevant retained lesson still applies. If a source is corrected or withdrawn, recheck downstream conclusions that depended on it.
5. Attribute improvement by layer. Record task outcome, whether the prior lesson was retrieved and changed the action, process or harness behavior, safety and regressions, and resource use where relevant. A change in one layer does not prove a gain in another; a justified no-change decision is valid. For a claim that retained experience caused a later benefit, compare matched later tasks with and without that state or use another adequate isolation design under controlled model, tools, and budget; otherwise leave attribution unresolved.

The transfer here is procedural. It does not implement the model-training or harness mechanisms in the cited work. Relevant primary research includes [AREX](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.21461v3) on targeted research from unresolved constraints, [OpenSkill](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.06741v1) on source-grounded skill acquisition, [Recuris](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.24876v1) on verified working state and validation-gated shared memory, [PAST-Bench](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.04003v1) on retained-experience pathway tests, [MetaSkill-Evolve](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.05297v1) on fast task and slow meta-skill updates, and [RRSI](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.24972v3) on overfitting and pruning. The [OpenAI mathematics release history](https://github.com/openai/math/blob/main/history.md) is a mutable index that illustrates why version and dependency changes need downstream review; pin a commit before using it as evidence for a specific claim. It does not prove a general RSI method.

## AI literature refresh

Before each material AI-related research or design cycle, actively search current AI literature that could change the problem framing, method, baseline, evaluation or safety decision. This applies across projects, including AI engineering and improvement of the agent's own workflow. Research relevant adjacent fields when they offer a different mechanism; do not limit discovery to a preferred lab, model family or the latest experiment's branch.

A material cycle means a meaningful hypothesis, method, evaluation or AI engineering decision, not every action performed by an AI. A repair within the checked plan can reuse its research unless it changes the assumptions, hypothesis or comparison.

- Start from the current question and the date of the previous review. Search newly published or revised papers, technical reports and authoritative implementation/evaluation updates since that review; retain relevant foundational work. Reuse prior reading and inspect meaningful changes instead of rereading the whole archive. Routine edits within a checked plan need only relevant change checks.
- Prefer primary papers and official technical artifacts. Read the methods, assumptions, results and limitations needed for the proposed use; inspect appendices or implementations when the claim depends on them. Record paper identity/version, publication or update date, reading depth and access limits. Abstracts, announcements, forums and social posts can reveal leads but do not establish that a paper was read or its claim verified. Recency, prestige and benchmark headlines alone are not evidence of quality.
- Seek strong ordinary baselines, contradictory findings, negative results and replication evidence alongside promising approaches. Translate a useful idea into a concrete mechanism, required information/resources, applicability conditions, a plausible failure and a discriminating check. Combine or adapt ideas when justified; distinguish a proposed transfer from a tested improvement.
- Update the existing source/decision record with search date and scope, relevant new or revised sources, and what changed in the plan or why it stayed unchanged. Preserve gaps and corrections; never claim exhaustive coverage of all new papers. If access is unavailable, record that limit and use permitted local evidence without labelling it a fresh external review. This rule does not override user restrictions or authorize spending, private-data uploads, installation or execution of retrieved code.

Research should affect decisions or resolve uncertainty. Do not add a separate report, an arbitrary paper-count quota or repeated broad searches for every command. New findings inform the existing plan and later tests; a literature citation alone does not demonstrate learning, novelty or improved performance.

## Research programmes and inventive prototypes

For an open-ended method, product or research programme, apply these checks at the start and close of every material cycle. Use the existing plan and cycle record; a brief decision is sufficient when nothing relevant changed. Do not wait for a user reminder or reload the whole archive for every command.

1. **Choose from the whole programme.** Recover the primary outcome, active mechanism, dependency path and strongest positive and negative evidence across relevant earlier work. For a material next-step recommendation, record the decision before presenting or launching it: the earlier evidence that matters, a materially different credible alternative, why the selected action advances the primary outcome, and the observation that would reverse the choice. Make this basis visible in the recommendation or link its concise current record. Reading the plan, naming a mechanism or adding citations is not evidence that the alternatives were compared. Explain why this next action advances the outcome more than repeating the last run's local variation. Distinguish delivering a component, improving the integrated system and improving the learning procedure. At closure, reconcile operative priorities, status summaries and next actions in place; date historical recommendations and point them to the active decision. More appended reports cannot repair a stale plan.
2. **Refresh knowledge that could change the design.** Check relevant new primary research and local findings under the core freshness rule. Map a useful idea to its assumptions, an executable operation or changed decision, a credible ordinary comparator, and a discriminating check. A theorem, analogy or another model's success can suggest a route; it does not establish this transfer. Preserve access and reading limits. Reuse a still-applicable review when no new uncertainty calls for another search.
3. **Explore a different mechanism when warranted.** For a new design choice or an unproductive path, compare the strong simple approach, the current candidate and a materially different or hybrid alternative. Change a representation, learning target, decomposition, information source or interaction when evidence supports it; another threshold or label is not automatically a new idea. Existing mechanism lists are revisable. Add, merge or retire a component when its distinct job, dependencies, expected value and test are explicit. Do not expand the task or add machinery merely to appear novel.
4. **Construct before demanding superiority.** Turn a safe authorized idea into a useful operation, derivation, prototype or counterexample test. Do not make an arbitrary baseline failure fraction, novelty verdict or adoption threshold a universal prerequisite for construction. Challenge apparent limits by inspecting their assumptions and proposing a different route; preserve valid constraints and negative evidence. After construction, compare with competent ordinary work using comparable useful information, tools and resources. State the contribution first, then its precise evidence and remaining limits without repeated anticipatory dismissal.
5. **Prevent an endless local search.** Carry the consecutive material-cycle count at the declared primary-outcome scope into the next handoff. A component pass, local closure or rewritten plan does not reset it; a direct test of that outcome can be informative even when negative. When continuing a narrowed branch after the workstream review, name the credible alternative being deferred and the concrete observation or construction that ends the bounded continuation. Repeated variants need a new decision-changing hypothesis and a stated connection to the programme. Apply the core stagnation review even when prerequisites pass or component metrics improve while the integrated objective remains unanswered. A justified continuation, integration, alternative or retirement is an outcome; forcing a positive result is not. Count cumulative learning and maintenance costs over the relevant horizon, without treating either an early overhead or hoped-for future amortization as the final answer.
6. **Let use change the method.** Carry the verified lesson into the next actual operation and revise the existing plan from its result. Keep source-bound task knowledge local; promote a transferable instruction defect through the separate shared-skill checks. Retain a working conventional component when it wins. If no mechanism or procedural change is justified, record that decision and continue the useful authorized work.

These are decision checks, not a requirement to invent an algorithm, browse, produce alternatives or write a report for every routine edit. They guide ambition and disciplined construction while preserving the user's objective, security boundaries and honest evaluation.

## When programme omissions recur

Use one current decision record in the governing programme, rather than another accumulating reminder. Keep source/version pointers and the reason for deferring each credible alternative. Preserve the primary-outcome progress counter; a local pass does not reset it. Recheck this record before the next material recommendation and before a study launch. Refresh only evidence whose change could alter the choice. If the same omission recurs, check whether the record was actually read and affected selection, and repair that delivery path.

A task-local checker may reject missing decision fields or stale source bindings where repeated omissions justify it. Such a checker validates declared structure and identity, not the quality or completeness of reasoning, and cannot enforce a host that never invokes it. Exercise the original omission, a different realistic decision and an ordinary action that should bypass the full review. Preserve user overrides and authority boundaries. Claims of reduced drift or better later outcomes require subsequent behavioural evidence.
