# Method selection, retained experience and limits of correction

19 October 2026 · Exploratory study and engineering evidence

REL can support useful methods and adaptation without reliably correcting every
recurring error. This study separates a compact method selector, learning through
successive tasks, one procedure revision and a second-host operation check. The
optional library in this delivery remains experimental; no universal, sustained
or exponential improvement is established.

## What was compared

The baseline was the private package at commit
`c1346cd3123c9c3ec2b443a3a4ab593fd3391aa7`, entrypoint SHA-256
`17e4c092a9323099194d05664b0ffff3afd8ff3f2758daa24a5f07344ea0991e`.
The frozen S candidate added a 76-word selection paragraph and an optional
context/method/output reference. All native comparison arms could access the same
neutral catalogue of 18 method families. S2 later added exactly 90 words about
reusing already complete, pinned observations, with explicit recovery and
verification exceptions. The delivered package instead adds one conditional
link and portable references; its bytes are not either tested candidate.

Native workers used gpt-6-luna/medium, fresh sessions, the same task/tool contract,
60-action and 900-second allowances, and bounded offline executable evaluation.
The later comparisons all used receipt-only feedback without an extra reflection
turn. Cases used a bounded [CloudEvents 1.0.2](https://github.com/cloudevents/spec/blob/v1.0.2/cloudevents/spec.md)/[RFC3339](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc3339) reconciliation profile: source/id
identity, payload normalization, strict error classes, time windows, conflicting
events and physical delivery accounting. These are constructed cases with shared
specifications and generator families, not independent production datasets.
The [CloudEvents JSON format](https://github.com/cloudevents/spec/blob/v1.0.2/cloudevents/formats/json-format.md) informed null and payload semantics; conflict, window, count and output policies were researcher-authored. Current rules were supplied to every arm. Private evaluation gold was inaccessible through worker
tools but readable by the research coordinator; this was not a hostile sealed
evaluation. Independently implemented checks qualified the expected outputs.

Two separate fresh subagents also completed a 240-row shipping-document task and
a simple exact-count task with the frozen selector. The shipping task required
reading an image-based exception, preserving physical rows, decimal arithmetic,
complete JSON and a concise explanation of an unavailable manual. Both engineering
checks passed. They had no matched controls or complete token accounting, and do
not establish comparative benefit or domain transfer of a learning effect.

## M2: correct common experience and method selection

One acquisition worker built the actual starting executable and authored lessons.
It passed 19/19 cases and used 757,039 recorded tokens including feedback. A
separate ordinary development worker passed 19/19 using 349,836 tokens. These
roles had different feedback policies and were not a randomized acquisition
comparison. Replaying the unchanged acquired code passed all 21 compatible cases
but only 3/22 under each of two changed policies, establishing real reasons to
reuse, adapt or replace policy. This replay made no model calls.

Nine later native sessions received the same origin code, lessons and raw
feedback. O was ordinary work, R the frozen REL baseline and S the selector.

| Current contract | Ordinary O tokens | REL R tokens | Selector S tokens | Correctness in every arm |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Compatible reuse | 181,867 | 272,891 | 203,914 | 21/21 |
| Multi-source adaptation | 532,647 | 560,592 | 375,625 | 22/22 |
| Delivery-policy replacement | 519,257 | 543,619 | 201,522 | 22/22 |
| Three-task total | 1,233,771 | 1,377,102 | 781,061 | 3/3 complete tasks per arm |

S used 43.3% fewer total recorded tokens than R and 36.7% fewer than O. Excluding
cached input, the reductions were 13.9% and 26.3%. Ordinary work was cheaper on
the reuse task. This is one attempt per arm/task, with one common origin and a
correctness ceiling. It justified another exploratory stage, not a general
efficiency or accuracy claim. The common acquisition is recorded once in actual
costs; allocating it to a counterfactual policy comparison does not spend it again.

## M3: each arm must build and carry its own method

The next comparison started without that common implementation. O and S each
completed a six-episode sequence with their own saved code and raw feedback,
fresh worker contexts, a restart and changing requirements. Requirements included
multi-source rollups, counting physical deliveries instead of deduplicating,
multiple named windows and filtering before conflict grouping. Both could retain
the same kinds of information; neither saw the sibling arm's history.

| Own-history policy | Fully correct episodes | Case outputs passed | Total recorded tokens | Input not cached + output |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Ordinary O | 0/6 | 29/36 | 2,642,849 | 294,817 |
| Selector S | 0/6 | 30/36 | 2,664,188 | 307,196 |

The selector used 0.8% more total tokens and 4.2% more input-not-cached plus output.
Tokens per fully correct episode are undefined because neither arm completed one.
This is one matched history pair, not twelve independent sequences or 72
independent tasks. No unchanged expansion was admitted.

Both arms adapted the major policies correctly in the evaluated interactions.
Both corrected the oversized-integer classification in episode two. Ordinary work
nevertheless retained an extra null timestamp member throughout; the selector
continued classifying a valid JSON decimal as a JSON syntax error rather than the
declared domain-validation error. The errors persisted through episode six.

History custody passed. Each final archive contained 54 entries, totaling
1,012,503 bytes for O and 678,583 for S. Those are retained bytes, not tokens loaded
or beneficial knowledge. The lesson files were intentionally empty host receipts
under receipt-only feedback; actual learner code and raw outcomes were retained.
They must not be described as learner-authored prose lessons.

Trace review identified a concrete separation between availability and use. Some
feedback reads stopped before the observed result; one extraction guessed an
absent field, printed a large input until truncation and then failed on a type
assumption. The selector's second episode did expose relevant expected and
observed values, yet repaired only one of the two differences. Better formatting
alone is therefore not an established solution. Compact, typed mismatch extraction
versus raw retention is a justified **untested** next question. It is not a new
mandatory rule or an admitted learning improvement.

## M4: a bounded change to the improvement procedure

A researcher proposed one 90-word observation-reuse paragraph from earlier
M2 traces. Fixed S and revised S2 started from the same real acquired method,
then retained only their own results through three later tasks: compatible reuse,
multi-source adaptation and changed delivery policy. Each task had eight cases
and 393–457 physical input items. The final paragraph and admission rule were
frozen before dispatch; it was not tuned against these outcomes.

| Procedure | Fully correct episodes | Case outputs passed | Total recorded tokens | Input not cached + output |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Fixed S | 1/3 | 21/24 | 1,705,059 | 155,107 |
| Revised S2 | 3/3 | 24/24 | 927,628 | 127,116 |

S2 used 45.6% fewer total recorded tokens and 18.0% fewer input-not-cached
plus output tokens. It used more of the latter on the final task: 68,919 versus
51,471. The common acquisition is reported separately; parent and reviewer effort
remain incompletely measured. This is one paired three-episode history in one
source family, not six independent learning trajectories.

Trace review found 12 versus 6 extra complete pinned `read` observations overall,
but the pipeline-only counts were 8 versus 6; the episode directions were mixed.
These counts exclude Python reads and mutable-output verification. S2 reread and
printed some input through Python, so the narrower count cannot establish total
I/O savings or the intended causal mechanism.

The fixed procedure introduced an extra null-timestamp member while adapting the
second task, despite initially correct code. The error remained in both final-task
failures. Feedback was accessed, but the attempted extraction did not diagnose
the nested-field difference. The revised procedure avoided this error in the
observed pair. The result supports retaining S2 as a **scoped research candidate**
under the frozen criterion, not installing it as the default or claiming better
autonomous improvement. The proposal was researcher-authored, not learner-created.

The exact candidate remains available for review:

> Reuse an available complete observation of a pinned, read-only input when its version and relevant range still cover the decision. Reasoning about that content again need not fetch it again. Re-fetch when the earlier observation is incomplete or unavailable, a needed range is missing, the version may have changed, or contradictory evidence requires a source check. Read the smallest sufficient range. Preserve mutable-artifact rereads after edits and before replacement when needed, required validation, exact constraints, source binding and the output contract. This adds no mandatory log, form or reflection pass.

## M5: actual operation through a second host

A cached Llama 3.2 3B model ran CPU-only through a Windows Ollama controller and
the existing offline Docker guest. Two fresh controller processes received the
actual acquired method and lesson. In each session the model first guessed a
nonexistent filename, recovered through the supplied handoff and invoked the
saved method on all 19 declared cases. All exact outputs matched in both sessions.
There were eight actual requests, with 28,914 reported prompt and 236 output
tokens. Both sessions ended at their four-request limits without final prose.
Thus the tool path worked, but complete user-workflow finalization was not shown.

The controller separately rejected a stale write and recovered an injected
interruption between activation and journal commit. This was researcher-induced
recovery, not an autonomous model repair. These checks used the baseline REL and
a study-local adapter, not the delivered selector on a second domain. They do not
establish broad portability, fresh capability acquisition or transfer of an
efficiency benefit. The adapter's private research dependencies are not copied
into the generic package.

## Corrections and delivered scope

Before dispatch, evaluator qualification caught and repaired two oracle defects;
the original failures remain archived. The source-bound worker accounting and
history-export checks reject unresolved or substituted records. A M3 family-label
preparation error and a sandbox-denied M4 qualification were preserved and
corrected before their affected model dispatches. They are not learner failures
or model trials.

The optional generic scoped-host example now rejects incomplete and length-limited
responses before executing their tool calls or accepting completion. Raw receipts
and usage remain recorded, with incomplete usage marked as a lower bound. Focused
tests cover truncation with pre-existing code and embedded writes. This is a
concrete protocol correction; the successful live M5 requests did not exercise
those negative branches and are not their validation.

The main skill gains one conditional link. Its optional selection reference and
18-family catalogue draw on selective loading and context/output separation,
including useful ideas from an available Hybrid Prompt Operator library. REL
requires neither that private library nor a fixed module bundle. Current
constraints, sources and deliverables remain authoritative. There is no automatic
extra routing call, reflection form, memory daemon or promise of weight changes.

## Costs, limitations and next decision

The complete native inventory contains **29 worker sessions**, not 29 model
requests: two acquisition/development sessions, nine M2, twelve M3 and six M4.
All terminal records, usage and history-custody checks reconciled. Recorded native
usage was **12,438,533 tokens**: 12,245,614 input
(including 10,999,040 cached) and 192,919 output.
Input not cached plus output totals 1,439,493.
Reported reasoning tokens are a subset of output, not an additional charge.
The separate M5 host reported 29,150 tokens across eight requests. The two M1
engineering subagents, parent research, review, preparation and human effort are
not fully accounted; currency, complete resource cost and physical energy were
not measured. These omissions are unknown costs, not zero. Summed worker wall
times overlap and must not be presented as campaign duration.

A prospective continuation amendment increased the native dispatch threshold
from six to twelve million recorded tokens, with the original attempts retained.
The final pair was admitted below that threshold and finished above it; already
admitted workers were allowed to finish. This was a dispatch threshold, not a
provider-enforced spending cap. No further trials were dispatched. Tokens are
neither subscription-quota percentages nor joules; cached and uncached totals
remain separate.

Case counts are workload coverage, not independent evidence of a population
effect. Known atomic regressions and recurring constructions remain declared.
These findings support specific operations, repairs and experimental resources.
They do not establish superiority to complete public RSI frameworks, a novel
learning algorithm, compounding benefit or universal effectiveness. The prior
manuscript's results remain historical; this dated study is a separate extension.

The next useful research question concerns turning a verified failure into the
correct later repair, including when relevant feedback is already visible. More
prompting techniques or another unchanged large batch would not answer it.
